# FCMS operations

## Configuration
Use Java 25 and PostgreSQL 18 (or the existing supported PostgreSQL installation). Set FCMS_DB_URL, FCMS_DB_USERNAME and FCMS_DB_PASSWORD outside source control. The production profile enables Secure session cookies; terminate HTTPS at the application or trusted proxy. Flyway applies forward migrations and Hibernate validates mappings.

Mail delivery uses Spring Boot's SPRING_MAIL_HOST, SPRING_MAIL_PORT, SPRING_MAIL_USERNAME and SPRING_MAIL_PASSWORD. The prod profile requires STARTTLS and verifies the SMTP server identity. Configure the provider and bounded connection/read/write timeouts (spring.mail.properties.mail.smtp.connectiontimeout, timeout and writetimeout). Set FCMS_MAIL_FROM through fcms.mail.from. Without a mail sender, outbox messages remain pending. Outbox delivery claims ten records, retries at most five times, clears sent and terminally failed message bodies and emits fcms.mail.sent/failed counters. Recovery tokens expire after fifteen minutes and five failed OTP attempts.

Configure fcms.backup.pg-dump with the installed pg_dump executable, and fcms.backup.directory with a private directory outside the executable web root. Backup work runs in one dedicated scheduler, accepts one outstanding job, has a ten-minute process timeout and records success/failure. Downloads require backup.manage. Store at most twenty completed snapshots before rotating them through an administrator-controlled operational process. Dumps contain sensitive data and must have filesystem access controls and encrypted storage. Restores are deliberate operator actions using pg_restore into an isolated database; never restore over the running database from a browser request. Uploaded contracts live outside the web root and accept PDF attachments up to five MiB.

## Authorization and scope
Roles grant claim bundles. user_claims adds per-user permissions; no business route checks role names. Company client registers and finance are explicitly claim-scoped. Staff tasks, leave and reports use ownership; group-report participants can read their report. *.read.all grants review visibility. Review/approval requires its own claim and independent actors. User permission/status changes and password changes revoke registered sessions after commit; service guards reload effective claims and account status.

## Financial state
Posted income, petty-cash, cloud and bulk-SMS entries are append-only. Corrections are independently authorized linked reversals. Account-row locks serialize balance/unit changes. Petty cash cannot overdraw; SMS stock cannot become negative. Request keys prevent repeated ledger posting, and changed payload reuse is rejected. Expenses move draft -> submitted -> approved/rejected -> paid -> reversed; approved/paid amounts cannot be edited. Client fee registers preserve the existing legacy revenue semantics and must not be interpreted as a general ledger. Cloud payment reports preserve the legacy positive-recorded-fee/zero-fee paid/unpaid classification.

## General ledger and accounting

The COA has five fixed classifications (Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Expenses and Income), six-digit immutable account codes, three heading levels and fourth-level posting accounts. Accounts are deactivated rather than deleted; active children prevent parent deactivation. Cash accounts must be asset posting accounts.

Configure the accounting opening date and policy reference, explicitly choosing ZERO or JOURNAL opening mode. A different actor must approve the books. JOURNAL mode requires a separately reviewed opening journal before ordinary posting and statements are available. No live opening balances are inferred or seeded. Periods cannot overlap, and unresolved draft/submitted vouchers prevent closing. Closing and posting coordinate through period-row locks.

Journals, receipts and payments contain 2–50 balanced decimal lines with evidence references, maker/checker review and explicit cash-flow classifications. Posted vouchers and their lines are immutable. Corrections use exact linked reversals with independent maker/checker controls; an original maker cannot check their own reversal. Reports read coherent posted snapshots; opening journals are start-of-day balances, not period income, cash flow or contributions. Statements remain identified as draft and expose unposted voucher counts and arithmetic reconciliation.

Activities and Transactions are the canonical posting configuration. V21 attaches every existing immutable template to its matching seeded transaction without rewriting the template payload or financial references. Activities and transactions are metadata only; no accounts, reviewed templates or journals are seeded. Setup creates or edits a transaction template, and an independent reviewer approves it. Approval atomically retires only the previous template of that transaction. Multiple transactions may serve one event; preparation requires an explicit eligible transaction when ambiguous. Disabling a transaction or its activity prevents new preparations, while already-prepared journals and exact linked reversals retain their original evidence. Old Posting mappings GET bookmarks redirect to the canonical transaction and exact template; obsolete POST commands return 410. COA, opening balances/books and periods remain distinct controls. Financial corrections continue to use compensating reversals.

## Reports
Screen registers use bounded server-side filtering, whitelisted sorts and deterministic identity ties. Operational PDF exports cap at 1,000 authorized records; narrow filters for larger datasets. The historical fee-and-expense summary aggregates all retained client fee records for the selected financial year, separate posted other income and paid expenses. Petty-cash transfers and cloud/SMS movements are not added to client fees a second time. Legacy CSV compatibility endpoints are retained for existing callers; the new UI uses PDF/print.

## Verification
Run mvn test for unit/architecture gates. Set FCMS_TEST_DB_URL to jdbc:postgresql://127.0.0.1:5432/fcms_codex_verify_<suffix> and FCMS_DB_USERNAME/FCMS_DB_PASSWORD to run destructive-fixture integration tests against a separate verification database only. The test guard refuses ordinary FCMS database names. Tests cover migrations, owner scope, claims, independent review, stale updates, immutable entries, retries and concurrent withdrawals. Runtime JSP smoke tests use an ephemeral local port.

## Rollout and rollback
Back up PostgreSQL and contract storage before rollout. Apply migrations on a copy of representative data first. V5 copies legacy tasks into work_items once. Existing expense IDs and client IDs are preserved. New ledgers start empty; migrating historical PHP transactions requires a reconciled import with provenance, not fabricated opening balances. Roll back application binaries only when schema compatibility has been assessed; financial/audit records must not be deleted. Prefer forward repair migrations.

## Capacity
No sustained 1,000-RPS claim is made. Define traffic mix, dataset, replicas and p95/p99 gates, then load-test with HTTP/JVM/Hikari/PostgreSQL metrics. The current local verification database is functional evidence only.

## Public directory read contracts
IAM owns iam_staff_directory (ID and display-name parts only). Clients owns client_saccos_directory (ID, name and visibility). Workforce/finance may join these two public read views to prepare bounded record DTOs; they must not query the underlying IAM/client tables or return internal entities. The owning public services validate chosen IDs for commands. Finance also owns finance_gl_cutover, finance_gl_sources and FinancePostingSourceService: a minimal company-scoped contract for immutable ledger movements, paid expenses and their reversals. It excludes client-fee snapshots and zero-value SMS stock changes. Missing historical expense payment dates remain unverified; integration must not invent dates or financial treatment. Common reference statuses/years are an explicit shared schema boundary.

## Accounting cutover integration contract

Book approval calls the public FinanceCutoverService in the same transaction. Finance owns the immutable operational cutover and rejects new originals dated before it. A shared advisory lock around operational insertion coordinates with exclusive cutover activation; the actual activation instant is retained. V18 has one explicit upgrade-only cross-module read of already-approved book date/policy/checker/time to initialize this Finance contract. Runtime Finance code reads its own cutover, not accounting internals. Source timestamps distinguish reviewed pre-opening records from later evidence that needs verification.

A source-bound voucher can be reviewed only by an actor independent of the source transaction maker. Expense payments identify the actual paid-by actor where retained, and reversals also exclude the original source actor. This adds GL evidence review without changing the existing operational expense approval/reversal lifecycle.

## Source coverage and period closing

V19 adds a Finance-owned closed-date contract and an accounting source-coverage projection. Period closing first locks the accounting period, then synchronizes the Finance guard under an exclusive source-write advisory lock. The same transaction refuses closure if any operational source through the period end remains unposted or unverified, including undated evidence. Finance source insertion, paid-expense evidence and reversals share that lock and reject closed dates. A failed close rolls back its mirror guard and audit records; reopening updates both modules atomically. The database also enforces coverage and matching Finance date guards on period-state changes.

Financial statement and trial-balance context separates source completeness from arithmetic reconciliation. All amounts can balance while operational sources remain missing. Missing source totals use absolute amounts so offsetting unposted transactions cannot conceal coverage gaps. Source queues retain undated evidence when filtering date ranges.

## Receipt/voucher and statement presentation

See docs/reused-accounting-ui.md for copied Microfinance source assets and FCMS adaptation. Formal voucher/statement PDFs use the existing OpenPDF library and the bundled SIL-licensed Noto font. At most two accounting PDF documents render concurrently per node. Statement previews drill to account contributions and then to exactly contributing journal lines, preserving opening/activity/closing and cash-flow filters. All details and exports retain accounting claims and company scope.

## Client archive and recovery
Client records are retained with their original identifiers, fee fields and contract references. Authorized users with client.read and client.write open Archive from the client register, inspect a record, give a reason and restore it through a version-checked POST. The same review path archives active records from Details. A successful retry does not create a second audit event; a conflicting version requires refreshed review. Company scope is explicit for these claims. Archive/restore records actor, operation, before/after visibility and reason. It does not post or reverse general-ledger money.

The legacy POST /clients/{type}/{id}/delete routes now redirect to archive review instead of changing visibility immediately. Existing integrations using those routes must follow the reviewed visibility command. No new schema or data rollback is needed for this slice; returning to an older application preserves the records but removes the new recovery UI.

Task managers without task.read.all can read, edit and manage tasks they created and assigned; they do not gain access to other managers' assignments. Removing task.manage immediately removes that creator-based scope. Owner/participant visibility remains unchanged.

Language links retain only approved navigation/filter keys. Password fields, CSRF tokens and request keys are excluded even when a form is redisplayed after validation failure.

## Historical payment evidence (V20)
The upgrade snapshots existing nonzero paid/reversed expenses with no supported payment date. New ordinary expenses cannot enter this legacy-candidate set. Zero-value expense history remains retained but creates no GL source. Payment evidence is stored separately; original expense amounts, dates, references, actors and timestamps are not rewritten.

The Payment evidence workspace requires expense.read plus expense.evidence.write or expense.evidence.review. These are company-scope financial permissions. They are included in the administrator bundle and may be assigned as separate custom claims. Payer lookup exposes only the public IAM directory ID/name and includes inactive historical identities; it does not grant those people access.

The maker supplies the supported payment date, actual payer, documentary reference and reason. The retained payment reference must match. A different reviewer, also distinct from the payer and original requester, approves/rejects; the maker can cancel a pending attempt. Rejected/cancelled attempts remain visible. Unique request keys and fingerprints protect creation retries; versions and retained review metadata protect review retries. Original/reversal source sequencing and independent GL posting still apply. Approval itself does not post a journal.

Approval coordinates with cutover/closing locks and rejects closed dates. A payment verified after cutover that predates opening must explicitly cite the already-approved opening policy. The public Finance source projection carries that reviewed coverage flag separately from its real verification timestamp. This supports opening-covered corrections without backdating evidence or assuming a prior journal. Resolve any genuinely closed historical date by the established period-reopening workflow before approval.

The only mutation lock order is expense row, Finance cutover/period boundary, then evidence row; it matches existing operational payment writes. Successful committed create/approve/reject/cancel operations increment fcms.expense.evidence with a bounded action tag. Retries and rolled-back commands do not count as successful operations. Audit events retain actor, action, expense/evidence identity, reason, timestamp and correlation ID.

V20 is a forward schema change with retained financial history. Application rollback must be assessed for its older source-view semantics: an older application will not understand reviewed legacy dates/opening flags. Do not drop approved evidence or restore an older database over new posted journals. Prefer a forward correction or compatible application build.

## Upload inspection and metadata commits
Profile images (2 MiB, actual PNG/JPEG, dimensions at most 4096 × 4096) and client contracts (5 MiB, actual unencrypted PDF, 1–500 pages) use bounded stream reads and private generated storage names. Scan/parse/write work runs before the short authorized metadata transaction. At most two image normalizations, two contract inspections and two scanner requests run concurrently per node; busy work is rejected rather than queued. The metadata save rechecks claims and record versions. A rolled-back save removes its newly staged file. An uncertain commit retains the file and increments fcms.upload.cleanup result=retained for operator reconciliation; cleanup errors increment result=failed. A process crash can also leave an unreferenced staged file: reconcile private storage against retained metadata/audit evidence before any cleanup. Replaced profile images are removed only after commit and when no user still references them. Replaced contracts remain retained, with old/new references in the audit event.

The prod profile requires scanning enabled; disabling it causes startup failure. Configure local clamd INSTREAM at 127.0.0.1 using FCMS_UPLOAD_SCAN_PORT (default 3310) and FCMS_UPLOAD_SCAN_TIMEOUT (default 5s, allowed 100ms–30s). The prod profile enables scanning; development may opt in using FCMS_UPLOAD_SCAN_ENABLED. Run the daemon on loopback, maintain its signature database and ensure its StreamMaxLength admits 5 MiB. Transport has a single overall deadline and bounded response size. Only an explicit clean response permits storage; scanner failures/timeouts deny the upload with retained form values. No filenames, file content or scanner signature details are logged. Metrics fcms.upload.scan use only bounded result tags clean/rejected/unavailable/busy/disabled. Protocol: https://docs.clamav.net/manual/Usage/ClamdProtocol.html . Verification uses a local synthetic protocol server; actual ClamAV installation/signature detection remains an environment acceptance check.

## Grouped chart of accounts
The COA presents five virtual classification groups following https://cloudscape.design/patterns/resource-management/view/table-with-grouped-resources/ . Actual account identifiers and parent relationships remain unchanged. A read-only repeatable-read transaction computes matching totals and retrieves at most size+1 accounts for each nonempty classification (10/25/50/100 per batch). There are at most six fixed read queries for the grouped data, rather than per-account queries. Child batches retain search/status/parent and whitelisted stable sorting. All five top-level groups fit the smallest group page. Selection of a group or all matching accounts includes collapsed/unloaded resources using in-memory selection predicates; no unbounded ID fetch or financial bulk command is introduced. Filters, sorting, refresh and history restoration clear selection. One selected loaded account enables View. No schema migration or financial posting behavior changes.

Navigation class/size changes, viewport resizing and scrolling refresh those bounds. Table preferences and discard confirmation synchronize immediately before opening. Blur/dimming stays clipped to the workspace and dialogs retain their existing focus/escape behavior. ## Transaction configuration upgrade
V21 is a forward migration. Take and verify a backup before applying it to a live database. Its ownership backfill uses a narrowly scoped trigger branch that permits only adding transaction_id to an existing template; the migration immediately replaces that branch with the normal immutable payload guard. Existing IDs, actors, approvals, hashes, source bindings and journal lines are retained. Configuration changes use optimistic versions, idempotency keys and retained audit records. Rollback requires a tested backup restore and matching application version; do not delete retained financial evidence or manually remove ownership columns.

## Production administrator bootstrap
The published admin@fcms.local development login is restricted to non-production use. Before activating the prod profile, prepare the database through an authorized account-management workflow in an isolated loopback-bound setup: give the retained administrator a unique deployment password, or provision another authorized administrator and disable the development account. Keep the existing account/claim audit trail; do not delete identities referenced by financial history. The application never generates, prints, rotates or disables a production credential automatically.

After migrations, a prod-only read check rejects the enabled V1 bootstrap identity when it still matches the published password, even if renamed or rehashed. It also rejects enabled copies of the published V1 hash or plaintext; the query returns at most two candidates. It does not perform an all-user bcrypt sweep. Independently, the production password encoder refuses authentication and encoding of the published raw password for every identity, covering salted copies and later reactivation/imports. Rejected password changes retain localized form errors. Transaction rollback preserves the account/challenge and existing after-commit session revocation rules. The normal prod Secure/HttpOnly/SameSite cookies and CSRF protection remain enabled. No bypass flag is supplied.

## Mail transport, leases and body retention
V22 removes body text from existing SENT/FAILED outbox rows without deleting their identity, recipient, subject, outcome, attempts or timestamps. New final failures and exhausted crashed-worker leases also clear bodies. Retryable PENDING/SENDING bodies remain until delivery or terminal failure. Request a new recovery challenge after a terminal failure; do not requeue a redacted terminal row. Back up before migration, and retain the redaction policy during application rollback. The migration does not alter password challenge hashes, user credentials, finance or audit records.

The trusted dispatcher claims at most ten records in a short transaction, performs SMTP outside the database transaction and acknowledges each attempt in another short transaction. Claims use SKIP LOCKED and a five-minute lease; stale attempts cannot acknowledge a reclaimed lease. SMTP is at-least-once: a crash after provider acceptance but before database acknowledgement may produce a duplicate notification. The one-use recovery challenge remains authoritative. Configure bounded SMTP timeouts so a batch fits its lease. fcms.mail.initial-delay defaults to 30000 milliseconds, as does fcms.mail.interval; the initial delay can be increased for controlled startup. Without an SMTP bean, messages stay pending. Local synthetic SMTP tests exercise real delivery, rejection, timeout, retries and database cleanup; external SMTP credentials, TLS and domain acceptance are deployment configuration.
