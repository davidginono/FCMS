# Business workspaces

The 17 November 2026 role bundles replace the earlier Admin/User defaults. HR is omitted as requested. Login dispatches through `/dashboard`; the displayed navigation uses effective claims, and controllers/services still enforce individual capabilities.

| Role | Home | Default access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounts Clerk | `/accounting/dashboard` | Transactions, accounting configuration, vouchers, financial reports, clients and sales |
| Staff | `/staff/dashboard` | Own leave requests and staff reports |
| Administrator | `/admin/users` | Users, personal account and system backups |
| Director | `/director/dashboard` | Financial reports, clients, sales, staff tasks/report assignment and independent report/leave review |

Workspace markers grant no capabilities by themselves. Per-user claim grants remain explicit overrides; personal actors retain the existing owner-scope ceiling unless deliberately granted a business workspace. No default role permits self-approval. The last-administrator protection and session revocation after account/permission changes remain active.

Create/Edit user initially checks the selected role's database-defined permissions. Every checkbox is editable and has no side badge. The submitted selection is authoritative: permissions beyond the role are stored as per-user grants, while unchecked role defaults are stored in `user_claim_denials`. The shared transactional `UserPermissionService` resolves exclusions for both login authorities and live request/service checks, retaining the established workspace/owner ceilings. Exclusions also prevent workspace fallback from restoring an unchecked workspace permission. Role switching keeps manual additions/exclusions in the live form; validation and edit rendering preserve the exact saved/submitted selection, including an empty selection.

The hidden `permissionsSubmitted` marker identifies the complete selection sent by current forms. Older open forms and grant-only service callers retain their earlier contract and cannot silently clear existing exclusions. Permission changes remain transactional, revoke affected sessions after commit and record per-claim before/after selection and exclusion states. The last-administrator guard requires an active account with the administrative workspace plus user-read and user-management capabilities, accounting for exclusions under the existing serialized write lock.

Their legacy domain fixtures/renderers remain internal and are no longer browser entry points or assignable permissions. Manual journals and existing source-linked preparation retain their own workflows.

## Reference business rules

The authorized live PHP system was inspected read-only: dashboard, client register/add form, petty cash and SMS purchase/sale forms. Its sanitized source was also inspected after verifying the archive checksum. Production records and credentials were not imported.

- SMS stock purchase: money out, units in. Client sale: money in, units out. Direction and category are supplied by the operation on the server. Unit-only adjustments and cash-only purchases remain supported; stock cannot become negative. Current units cover all dates. Amount in/out and profit cover the complete selected year/category/date/search result, independently of pagination. Recorded unit snapshots and financial reversal history remain unchanged.
- Petty cash: Deposit and Withdraw actions determine cash direction. Current balance covers all dates. Displayed row balances use transaction date and ID order, before applying search/date filters. Backdated entries do not rewrite stored historical posting snapshots.
- Clients: payment state remains explicitly stored, independently of fees paid; archived visibility remains separate from payment state. Dashboard client/fee totals use visible rows in the chosen fiscal year and category. SACCOS annual difference subtracts received amounts and withholding tax. Links open the canonical filtered register.
- Dashboard accounting income and expenses come from posted general-ledger lines. Unapproved/unopened books display unavailable totals. Retired legacy income/expense requests do not silently become posted accounting revenue or expense.

## User-form session recovery

Create/Edit user verifies the current authenticated administrator and refreshes the form's CSRF fields before its single native POST. The no-store probe requires `iam.user.manage`, returns only the current actor ID and CSRF token, and skips shell population. It never receives the form's fields or password. An expired session leaves the live draft in place and offers sign-in in another tab; submitting again is an explicit user action. A different signed-in actor, lost permission, timeout, draft edit or navigation cannot trigger submission. Verification is cancelled on edits and page exit. No private draft is stored in browser storage and no uncertain POST is replayed.

Older forms and JavaScript-disabled submissions with expired CSRF tokens retain HTTP 403 and show a localized sign-in/reopen page instead of Whitelabel. That page never echoes or replays submitted fields. Other authorization failures retain their existing behavior. This change requires no database migration.

## Upgrade and operations

Apply V30/V31 through Flyway. V30 replaces the four standard role bundles and preserves user claim overrides; existing accounts keep their role IDs. Review deliberate overrides when assigning a new standard role. V31 adds immutable configured-voucher evidence. Neither migration changes existing financial/client records, indexes, thread pools, external services or dependencies.

V32 adds the indexed per-user exclusion table; existing user, role and grant records are unchanged. Deploy through the normal Flyway startup and back up before upgrading. Prefer a forward fix. An older application ignores exclusions, so do not roll it back against a database with active exclusions without a coordinated permission/database restoration plan.

V33 removes obsolete permission catalog/assignment rows only; it changes no historical financial/client/workforce/audit rows, indexes, dependencies, pools or workers. Back up before upgrade. Rolling back the retired workspaces requires coordinated application and permission-data restoration from that backup; prefer a forward fix.
